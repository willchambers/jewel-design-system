The five Jewel colors

They make up the lava lamp animation in the background. Components never use them directly; charts use versions stepped for contrast.

Lava lamp palettes

The animation can use a warm or a cool set instead of Jewel’s own. Put data-palette on <html>. Text contrast doesn’t change: every palette color is darker than the near-white backdrop that glass is already tested over.

JeweldefaultWarm and cool together. The default.
Emberdata-palette="ember"Warm: reds, orange, amber and rose.

03 — Three tiers

How the tokens fit together

Primitives hold raw values. The theme maps them to colors. Components read only the resolved tier, which is what lets a solid panel or a future theme change their colors without touching a component.

TierWhereHolds
1. Primitives:rootThe Jewel palette, type scale, spacing, radii, motion
2. Theme[data-theme="dark"]--color-* and --glass-*
3. Resolved:root, [data-theme]--panel-*, --text-*, --field-*, --state-*, --chart-*

04 — Usage

Use it for

  • Resolved tokens only, in components and page CSS: --text-*, --panel-*, --field-*, --state-*, --status-*, --chart-*.
  • The accent for links on hover, focus rings, the current item and selected states.
  • The error red for errors only, on text and edges, and to fill the one destructive button.
  • Status colors for the state of things, always with a word beside them.
  • Overriding a token in your own stylesheet, loaded after jewel.css.

Avoid

  • Hex values or --color-* primitives in a component. They skip the glass and solid adjustments.
  • Colored backgrounds on notices and tags. Tone lives in a 2px edge. The destructive button is the one filled color.
  • Status colors as decoration, or for chart series.
  • A second accent color.
  • Changing a color without rechecking contrast in docs/accessibility.md.
