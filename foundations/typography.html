Typography

One typeface, Inter, does all the work. Hierarchy comes from size, weight and space, so the page reads calmly and the background can be loud.

01 — Typeface

Inter

Every heading, label and paragraph is set in Inter. Code uses the system’s monospace font.

Aa

Code
ui-monospace, SF Mono, Cascadia Mono, Roboto Mono, Menlo, Consolas

Principles

  • Hierarchy comes from size, weight and space, never from a second typeface.
  • One family, many voices: Inter at four weights covers display, headings, body and labels.
  • Body copy is 16px with a 1.6 line height, and lines stop at 64 characters.
  • Links carry a hairline underline that tightens on hover. Nothing else moves.
  • Captions and metadata step down in size and colour, not in family.
  • Labels are small, uppercase and widely tracked, so they read as metadata at a glance.

05 — Links and inline text

In running text

Links are the text colour with a hairline underline; on hover the underline tightens and the colour moves to the accent. .link-quiet hides the underline until hover, for wordmarks and lists of links.

Read the guide to tokens, or browse the component list. Link css/jewel.css, then press / to search. Use strong for the one phrase that matters, and emphasis sparingly.

06 — Usage

Use it for

  • One h1 per page, then headings in order. If a heading needs to look smaller, keep the right level and add .h3 or .h4.
  • .lede for the paragraph that introduces a page.
  • .label for metadata: dates, categories, numbered section heads.
  • .caption for notes under figures, tables and forms.

Avoid

  • A second typeface, or a weight outside 400 to 700.
  • Colour for emphasis. The accent is for links and focus, not for highlighting words.
  • Uppercase paragraphs. Uppercase is for short labels only.
  • Picking a heading level for its size. Screen readers use the levels to navigate.
