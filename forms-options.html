Three ways to draw a field.

The same small form in three directions: text, long text, select, checkbox and a tag. Each is shown empty, focused, filled, in error and disabled. The panel, type and labels are unchanged; only the field treatment differs.

A — Underline only

Up to 80 characters.

Street

Strengths

  • The lightest of the three. The type leads, and the underline echoes the section rules.
  • Works in any width, including the narrow tearsheet on phones.
  • Focus is a 2px accent underline: obvious, but quiet.

Trade-offs

  • The underline must be the brighter 40% line to meet 3:1, so it reads a step brighter than the panel hairlines.
  • A textarea looks like a single line until you type, so it relies on its height and the label.
  • The drop zone and tag field need their own shapes.

B — Hairline box

Up to 80 characters.

Street

Strengths

  • The most familiar shape: nobody wonders where to click. The clearest hit areas.
  • Square corners match the panels. A faint 3% fill gives each field a surface.
  • Suits the drop zone and tag field naturally, since they're boxes already.

Trade-offs

  • The heaviest of the three. A stack of boxes inside a glass panel adds a second layer of frames.
  • Four bright edges per field make a long form busy compared with the thin-rule look.
  • The most generic: closest to any other design system.

C — Editorial rows

Up to 80 characters.

Street

Strengths

  • The most distinctive, and the most "Jewel". It reads like the meta list and the section heads, like a colophon.
  • Very calm: one rule per row and no frames, so long forms stay quiet.
  • Focus lights up the whole row's rule in accent, which is easy to track.

Trade-offs

  • The label column takes width, so on phones it stacks the label above the field, which is really option A.
  • Tall items such as the textarea, drop zone and preview break the row rhythm.
  • Row rules must be the 40% field line, a step brighter than the .meta list's hairlines.

Recommendation

A, the underline, with C's rows as a layout option.

A is the closest match to how the system already speaks: type first, one hairline doing the work, and nothing boxed that doesn't need to be. It holds up in a narrow tearsheet on a phone, where C falls back to A anyway, and it stays quiet in long forms, where B gets busy.

C is lovely for short, settings-style forms, and it's cheap to offer as a layout modifier (.form--rows) on top of A's fields, since the field states are identical. B's box language is still right for the two components that are regions rather than lines: the photo drop zone and the tag field.

Pick A, B or C, or a mix, and Phase 2 builds the full set in that direction.
